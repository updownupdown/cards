Solitarius · three design directions

One app, three ways to tie it together

Museum art, quotes from real thinkers, the Tao, a voyage through space and a table of cards all have to feel like one dark, calm, sophisticated app without getting harder to use. Each direction below is a clickable prototype of every part of the app. They use the new cover art and the real quotes, portraits and constellations, with 40 games won.

ARisk: low

Nocturne

The museum after dark. What you've earned is lit.

  • Grows out of the Night Gallery: Instrument Serif and Outfit, with lamp gold as the only accent
  • Collect becomes Gallery, with a room per category and no stamps
  • The Tao and the quotes are "reading rooms" off the Voyage, drawn in lamp light
  • A win switches the painting's light on

BRisk: medium

Folio

A finely set book of games, with plates and an atlas.

  • One typeface (EB Garamond) with rubric red for actions and gilt for rewards
  • The Tao and the quotes merge into one Commonplace Book
  • The campaign becomes an engraved star atlas; search becomes an Index
  • Winning prints a plate; games open with an epigraph
Open Folio →
CRisk: medium–high

Prism

The art is the interface, and it sets the colour.

  • Each screen takes its tint and accent from the artwork in front of you
  • Three tabs. Collect and Search merge into Library, and the gallery is the catalogue
  • The table is the painting, blurred; card backs are the art
  • A win un-blurs the painting into full view

If I were picking

Nocturne as the base, with the best idea from each of the others

Nocturne is the safest to ship in steps and already matches the gallery you liked. Two ideas from the other directions solve problems it leaves open.

  1. Use Nocturne's tokens everywhere first ANight surfaces, one lamp accent, Instrument Serif for names and Outfit for controls. Then retire the red, blue, green and purple buttons. This alone fixes most of the incoherence.
  2. Merge Collect into the game browser CA gallery that is also the catalogue removes a tab and the stamps, and makes the art the reward everywhere. Search stays as a filter bar on top.
  3. Tint each table with its art CThe game's own dark colour as the lamp over the table, with a faint trace of its painting. It uses data you already have and needs no new backgrounds.
  4. Epigraphs and one sky BShow a reward quote where a game opens, so the quotes live in the app and not only in a drawer. Draw the Tao figures and the campaign map in the same style so they read as the same sky.
